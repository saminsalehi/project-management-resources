# Interior Fit-Out and Millwork Coordination Checklist

*Samin Salehi, PMP ([ORCID](https://orcid.org/0009-0005-4143-2816)) · Project Manager Land*

> General industry practice for coordinating architectural millwork within commercial interior fit-outs. It is a starting point only. Always follow your contract documents, specifications, and the standards that apply in your jurisdiction.

## Pre-construction
- [ ] Millwork scope is confirmed against the drawings, specifications and addenda
- [ ] Inclusions and exclusions are clear (hardware, countertops, glazing, electrical cut-outs, installation, blocking)
- [ ] Quality standard named in the specification is identified
- [ ] Long-lead materials are identified (specialty veneers, solid surface, stone, hardware)
- [ ] Millwork activities are in the general contractor's master schedule

## Shop drawings and submittals
- [ ] Shop drawing schedule agreed with the design team
- [ ] Samples and finishes submitted for approval
- [ ] Review comments tracked to closure in a submittal log
- [ ] Approved drawings released to production. Fabrication starts only after approval.

## Site coordination
- [ ] Site measurements taken where dimensions depend on built conditions
- [ ] In-wall blocking and backing confirmed before drywall closes
- [ ] Coordination with electrical, plumbing and data trades for openings and cut-outs
- [ ] Site readiness confirmed before delivery (finishes complete, area clean, conditioned, secure)
- [ ] Delivery, hoisting and storage logistics agreed with the general contractor

## Installation and closeout
- [ ] Installed work is protected from other trades
- [ ] Deficiency (punch) list walked and items assigned
- [ ] Deficiencies corrected and signed off
- [ ] Maintenance information and warranties handed over

## Risks to watch
- Late design approvals push fabrication into the installation window
- Site conditions don't match the drawings
- Sequencing conflicts with other finishing trades
- Damage to finished millwork after installation
