# The Five Project Management Basics

*By Samin Salehi, PMP · Adapted from the Project Manager Land video [PMP Exam Prep: Project Management Essentials](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kqo0o0_QQKU) (Oct 2026)*

A project can have a plan, a team and a schedule and still end up late, over budget or delivering the wrong result. Good project management is more than keeping people busy. You need to know what the project must deliver, what work comes first, who makes decisions, and what to do when something changes.

This guide uses **one running example**: *a small business wants a new website.*

---

## Basic 1: Be clear about the goal

"Build a website" is a **task**. The **goal** is what the business needs the site to do. For example: *help new customers find the business and book an appointment.*

A clear goal also tells you how to check the result:
- Can customers find the service they need?
- Can they book an appointment?
- Does the owner agree the site is ready?

**Ask three questions at the start:**
1. Why are we doing this?
2. What result do we need?
3. How will we know it's done well?

## Basic 2: Define the work (scope)

Agree on what the project will deliver. For the website: a home page, a list of services, contact details and a booking feature.

Also say what is **not** included. For example, the team builds the website but doesn't write a new business plan or set up a new payment system.

> **Scope** = what the project will and won't deliver.

If people don't agree on scope, new requests keep adding work. Write down the key deliverables, confirm that the people who approve them agree, and then break the work into smaller tasks.

## Basic 3: Plan the work and the time

1. Break the deliverables into smaller tasks.
2. Put them in order. The page design must be agreed before the pages are built, and the pages must be built before they can be tested. Each of these is a **dependency**: one task needs another to finish first.
3. Estimate how long each task may take and put the key dates on a schedule.
4. Keep it realistic. When a task runs late, check which later work it affects.

Some tasks have room for delay and others don't. If a task on the **critical path** slips, the finish date moves too.

> A schedule isn't just a list of dates. It shows the team what needs to happen next and where a delay would matter.

## Basic 4: Work with the right people

Every project involves people who do the work, people who make decisions and people who approve the result. For the website, one person writes the content, another builds the pages, and the owner approves the final design.

Be clear about three things:
- **Who** is doing each task?
- **Who** can make the decision?
- **Who** needs to approve it?

The project manager keeps people connected. That doesn't mean sending everyone every detail. It means sharing **the right information with the right people at the right time**, and making it clear when a decision is needed.

## Basic 5: Watch for risk and change

- A **risk** is something that *might* happen and affect the project.
- An **issue** is a problem that *has already* happened.

*Example:* the owner may be late sending photos for the website. Before the deadline, that's a risk. Once the deadline passes and the photos still haven't arrived, it's an issue.

Ask: *What could happen? What would it affect? What can we do now?* For example, request the photos earlier, or use temporary images in the meantime.

**Change needs the same attention.** If someone asks for a new feature, check its effect on the work, the schedule and the budget *before* adding it. Then the right people decide.

> You can't prevent every problem, but you can spot risks earlier, talk about changes and help the team choose its next step.

More detail: [Risk vs. Issue vs. Change](04-risk-issue-and-change.md)

## All five together

| Basic | Website example |
|-------|-----------------|
| 1. Goal | Help customers learn about the business and book an appointment |
| 2. Scope | Home page, service details, booking page (no business plan, no payment system) |
| 3. Plan | Agree content and design → build pages → test the site |
| 4. People | Writer prepares content, designer builds pages, owner reviews and approves |
| 5. Risk and change | Late photos → assess the schedule impact. Extra feature → check the impact before adding |

**Recap:** a clear goal guides the work, a clear plan helps people coordinate, and regular updates help the team respond when things change.

### Try it
You don't need a complicated system to start. For your next project, write down these five things and talk them through with your team.
