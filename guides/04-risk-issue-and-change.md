# Risk vs. Issue vs. Change: A Practical Guide

*By Samin Salehi, PMP ([ORCID](https://orcid.org/0009-0005-4143-2816)) · Builds on the examples in [Guide 1](01-what-is-project-management.md) and [Guide 2](02-five-project-management-basics.md)*

New project managers often mix up three things that need different handling.

| | **Risk** | **Issue** | **Change** |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Something that *might* happen and affect the project | A problem that *has already* happened | A request to alter scope, schedule, budget or approach |
| Time frame | Future | Present | Proposed |
| Where you record it | Risk register | Issue log | Change request / change log |
| Your first question | What could happen, and what would it affect? | What is it affecting right now? | What is the impact on work, schedule and budget? |
| Typical action | Reduce, avoid, transfer or accept, and plan a response | Resolve, work around or escalate | Assess, then the right people approve or reject |

## The same event, before and after

From the website example in [Guide 2](02-five-project-management-basics.md):
- **Before the deadline:** "The owner may be late sending photos." This is a **risk**. Response: ask for the photos earlier and prepare temporary images.
- **After the deadline passes:** "The photos haven't arrived." This is now an **issue**. Response: use the temporary images, update the schedule and tell the people affected.

From the patient room example in [Guide 1](01-what-is-project-management.md):
- **"Equipment delivery will be late."** First, find out what it affects. Can other work go ahead? Does the ready date move? Who needs to decide? Then bring the right people together and give the people who depend on that date a clear update.
- **A guardrail:** a delay is never a reason to skip a required check. The plan can change, but the result still has to meet the project's needs.

## A five-step response routine
1. **Name it.** Is it a risk, an issue or a change?
2. **Assess the impact** on scope, schedule, cost, quality and safety.
3. **Identify options.** Re-sequence work, add resources, use a temporary workaround, or change a date.
4. **Get a decision** from the person who has authority (see the [RACI](../templates/raci-matrix.csv)).
5. **Communicate and record it.** Update the plan and tell everyone who depends on it.
